# What to measure, study or show next (5 Oct 2026)

**Asked by the owner:** "From the data we already hold, what should we measure, study or show next?"
**Written by:** the quant-advisor agent. A proposal, not a decision: the owner picks, the
technical lead writes the spec.

**Already done or planned, so not repeated here:** breadth (NIFTY 50, whole market, index
lists), the washout alarm, volume and delivery studies 0002–0005 (no buy/sell edge; big jumps,
not volume, go with the later lag), Unusual activity, Top volume, Money flow and its history.
The TODO already holds the breadth thrust and divergence studies, the momentum study before the
Strength Leaderboard, and 52-week highs vs lows. Where an idea below touches one of those, it
says what to change about it.

**How sure are the numbers below?** Every number comes from a read-only query run today. Some
are deliberately rough (raw prices, every stock counted) and are marked "rough": they show
roughly how big the effect or the sample is. They are not results.

## Ranked ideas

Ranked by how much each helps you see where money moves, divided by the work it takes.

### 1. "Where is the money going?" Big stocks vs the long tail (tracking)

**What it is.** Each day, split the ₹ traded on NSE into three groups: the 100 most-traded
stocks, the next 400, and everything beyond 500. Chart each group's share since 2016.

**Worked example (query, series EQ, by calendar year):**

| Year | Top 10 stocks' share | Top 100 share | Beyond the top 500 | Total ₹ traded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | 25.6% | 77.2% | **1.6%** | ₹83.6 lakh crore |
| 2021 | 18.8% | 65.5% | 5.8% | ₹170.4 lakh crore |
| 2024 | 13.1% | 52.1% | 11.2% | ₹286.1 lakh crore |
| 2026 (to 1 Oct) | 13.2% | 47.8% | **14.7%** | ₹228.8 lakh crore |

In plain words: in 2019, 98 rupees in every 100 went into the 500 most-traded stocks. In 2026,
nearly 15 rupees in every 100 go into stocks outside them. That shift, from the big names to
the long tail of small companies, is exactly "where money moves", and nothing in the app shows
it today.

- **Data:** `daily_prices` (turnover), `fund_symbols` (leave ETFs out).
- **Tracking or predictive:** tracking. Ships as facts.
- **Prior that a study would pass:** not applicable. A predictive version ("when the long tail's
  share peaks, small caps fall") is tempting, but 2016–2026 holds two or three such cycles at
  most, far too few to test. Park it; never imply it on the page.
- **Main traps:**
  - The groups must be decided **on each day** from the previous ~3 months of trading (point in
    time), not from today's NSE size lists. Otherwise today's winners get placed in the past.
  - Call them "most traded", not "large cap". We don't hold shares outstanding, so we can't
    measure company size (market value).
  - The number of listed EQ stocks grew from about 1,700 to 2,900, so the long tail is a
    growing group. Show the stock count per group beside the share.
  - Muhurat and other short sessions distort single days (`short_sessions` already detects
    them). Weekly or 20-day smoothing avoids it.
- **Effort:** about 1 day (one derived table plus a chart; it can live on Money flow or Top
  volume).

### 2. New 52-week highs vs new lows, whole market (tracking)

**What it is.** Each day, count how many liquid stocks closed at their highest price in a year,
and how many at their lowest. It is the simplest "how many stocks are doing well" gauge, and it
works for the whole market.

**Worked example (rough: raw prices, every EQ stock with a full year of history):**
26 Sep 2024: 116 highs, 14 lows, out of 1,439 stocks. 3 Mar 2025: 6 highs, **513 lows**,
out of 1,696. 1 Oct 2026: 33 highs, 187 lows, out of 2,117.

- **Data:** `daily_prices` through `loadAdjustedHistory` (renames followed, splits adjusted),
  the ₹1 crore liquidity rule, `fund_symbols`.
- **Tracking or predictive:** tracking. It is already in TODO "Nice-to-haves". I would move it
  up: it is cheap, and it is the whole-market companion to breadth.
- **Prior:** not applicable as shown. Using highs minus lows to time the market is a long shot
  (few independent episodes; research 0001 found a similar breadth extreme told us nothing).
- **Main traps:**
  - Unadjusted prices. A 1:10 split looks like a 90% crash and would invent a "new low". Must
    use adjusted history.
  - Don't use NSE's 52-week high/low file. It is off by one day (CLAUDE.md).
  - Holes in the price history (`segmentByGaps`): a stock back from a long suspension has no
    honest one-year window.
  - Count only liquid companies, or illiquid stocks will dominate the lows.
  - The first full year of history ends around Sep 2017, so the chart starts there.
- **Effort:** about 1 day. Side benefit: each stock's distance from its 52-week high is the
  input for idea 3's leaderboard.

### 3. Momentum study, whole market: do last year's winners keep winning? (predictive)

**What it is.** At each month-end, rank every liquid company by its return over the past 12
months, skipping the most recent month. Then check whether the top tenth beat the bottom tenth
over the next month. This is the study the TODO already asks for before the Strength
Leaderboard. My advice is to run it on the **whole liquid market**, not the NIFTY 50: 50 stocks
is too few to rank.

**Worked example (our own `index_prices`, 28 Sep 2016 → 1 Oct 2026, price indices):**
NSE's *Nifty Alpha 50*, a live index of recent outperformers (close cousin of momentum), rose
**18.6% a year** against 11.3% for the Nifty 500 and 13.3% for the Smallcap 250. Since Aug 2020
the *Nifty200 Momentum 30* rose 15.5% a year against 13.7% for the Nifty 200. *Nifty100 Low
Volatility 30* earned about the same as the Nifty 100 (10.4% vs 10.1%) with smaller swings.
This is one index each, before costs. It is a reason to test, not a result.

- **Data:** `daily_prices` via `loadAdjustedHistory`, `fund_symbols`, the liquidity rule.
- **Tracking or predictive:** predictive, so it needs a study with rules fixed first.
- **Sample (query):** one ranking per month. Discovery has about 63 month-ends (Sep 2017 to
  Nov 2022), each with roughly 730–1,350 liquid companies. The hold-out has about 44 month-ends
  (2023 onward), with roughly 1,500–1,900 each. (Counts use average turnover ≥ ₹1 crore by year,
  so they are approximate. The study uses the project's median rule.)
- **Prior: medium that momentum is real here, low-to-medium (about 1 in 3) that it clears our
  bar.** Momentum is one of the best-documented effects in Indian shares (Sehgal & Balakrishnan
  2002; Ansari & Khan 2012; NSE built indices on it). But our bar asks for a Fama–MacBeth
  t ≥ 3 on only ~63 discovery months. (Fama–MacBeth: fit the effect separately for each month,
  then ask how consistently it shows up across months.) Even a strong strategy earning twice its
  risk per year would reach only about t ≈ 2.3 over 5 years. **A fail would most likely mean
  "too little history to prove it", not "momentum is false".** You need to know that before it
  runs.
- **Main traps:**
  - **Survivorship:** include companies that later delisted. `daily_prices` keeps them, which
    is good. A stock that stops trading during its holding month must count, not drop out.
  - **Look-ahead:** liquidity and eligibility measured with data up to the ranking date only.
  - **Skip the latest month:** last month's big movers tend to reverse (consistent with what
    0005 found about big jumps). Including it would muddy the result.
  - **Overlapping returns:** use non-overlapping monthly holding periods.
  - **Momentum crashes:** Apr–May 2020's rebound punished last year's winners. Report it,
    never trim it.
  - **Costs and circuits:** a monthly-rebalanced top tenth trades a lot. Small caps carry
    impact cost and STT, and a stock locked at its upper circuit can't be bought. Use the
    0.5 pts/month cost bar and report results with and without the smallest third.
  - **Multiple testing:** test momentum alone, or momentum plus low volatility as two
    pre-declared factors. Nothing more.
- **What each outcome changes:** pass → the Strength Leaderboard can carry one sourced sentence
  ("since 2017, the top-ranked tenth beat the bottom tenth by X pts a month, study 0006").
  Fail or "not settled" → the leaderboard still ships, as plain ranks with no "winners keep
  winning" claim.
- **Effort:** 2–3 days (spec, study and write-up). The regression and luck-check code from
  0004/0005 can be reused.

### 4. Re-scope the breadth thrust and divergence: whole market, history table, not an alarm (tracking)

**What it is.** The TODO plans to study Zweig's breadth thrust on the NIFTY 50 (the share of
rising stocks over 10 days goes from under 40% to over 61.5%). With 50 stocks it fires on
noise. On the ~900–1,900 liquid companies it behaves as designed, **but it is rare**.

**Worked example (rough: 10-day simple average of rising share, liquid EQ stocks, raw
prev_close):** it fired in about **7 separate episodes** since Oct 2016: Oct 2016, Jan 2017,
Apr 2020, Apr 2024, Jun 2024, Dec 2024, May 2025.

- **Data:** `daily_prices` (day moves computed on adjusted history for every symbol, not
  bhavcopy's `prev_close`), `index_prices`, `breadth_daily` (whole-market % above the 200-day
  average since Jul 2017, about 870 stocks on average).
- **Tracking or predictive:** **tracking.** Seven events cannot pass any luck check at 97.5,
  whatever happens after them. So don't spend a formal study on it. Show it the way the washout
  alarm is shown: every past occasion and what the NIFTY 50 did after each one, labelled "7
  occasions, not a test" (as decision 0017 did).
- **Divergence:** same advice. Chart NIFTY 50 breadth and whole-market breadth together (both
  are already in `breadth_daily`) and show the gap as a fact. Don't fit X and Y thresholds on a
  handful of market tops; that is tuning by another name.
- **Prior of a formal pass:** low. The sample is too small, not the idea too weak.
- **Main traps:**
  - Day moves need `change_pct` for every symbol. Today it exists only for index members (a
    TODO prerequisite), and `prev_close` is not adjusted on ex-dates.
  - Short Muhurat sessions can flip a 10-day average.
  - The liquidity rule must be measured on each day.
- **Effort:** half a day to a day once whole-market day moves exist. Removes two studies from
  the TODO.

### 5. Sector strength from NSE's own sector indices, beside Money flow (tracking)

**What it is.** For each NSE sector index, show its price return over 1, 3 and 6 months against
the Nifty 500, next to Money flow's "₹ traded vs normal". Then you see both whether money is
pouring into a sector and whether its price is following.

**Worked example (query, last session on or before 1 Jul 2026 → 1 Oct 2026):** Nifty IT
+9.8%, Pharma +4.5%, Metal +0.5%, Nifty 500 −5.4%, Bank −6.2%, Energy −8.5%, FMCG −12.1%.
IT beat the market by about 15 points in three months. FMCG trailed it by about 7.

- **Data:** `index_prices` (every NSE sector index, daily, since 2016), `money_flow`,
  `sector_flow_weeks`.
- **Why this way:** Money flow's sectors come from today's index lists (decision 0028 says
  so), which is right for "now" but wrong for history. NSE's sector index closes were computed
  on each day's real members, so a 52-week chart built from them is honest about the past.
- **Tracking or predictive:** tracking. Sector momentum (Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999) is real
  in US data, but with ~15 Indian sectors and ~63 discovery months a test is weak. Fold it into
  idea 3 later as a side question, if at all.
- **Main traps:** these are price indices (dividends left out; FMCG and PSU banks pay more, so
  they look a little worse than they are). Sector indices overlap (Bank vs PSU Bank vs
  Financial Services), so don't add them up. A few indices started after 2016, so their charts
  start later.
- **Effort:** about 1 day.

## Parked (needs data we don't hold)

## The one thing I would do first

**Idea 1, big stocks vs the long tail.** It takes a day and needs no study. It answers your
core question ("where does the money go?") with a fact the app doesn't show: in 2019, under 2%
of NSE trading went outside the 500 most-traded stocks, and in 2026 almost 15% does. After that,
start the momentum study (idea 3), knowing beforehand that our short history may make
"not proven" the most likely verdict even if momentum is real.
