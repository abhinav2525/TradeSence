Does volume predict the next month?

We tested six popular volume signals on 2,367 NSE companies that traded ₹1 crore+ a day (ETFs left out). Each signal is compared with other stocks on the same days, decided on 2016–2022 and checked again on 2023–2026.

The answer

Two kinds of day were followed by stocks doing worse than others, in both periods. Both are big up days, and big one-day jumps often give back ground whatever the volume, so this shows what followed these days, not that volume caused it. The four other signals were too small or not proven. "pts" is the gap in percentage points between those stocks' return and other stocks' return on the same days.

How to read this

  • "pts" means percentage points: the gap between two returns. If signal stocks returned −0.7% in a month and other stocks on the same days +0.2%, the gap is −0.9 pts.
  • Facts, not tips. "Behind by 0.9 pts" is a typical result over thousands of cases. Plenty of single stocks did the opposite.
  • The surprise is the direction. Heavy volume on an up day and heavy-volume breakouts are usually read as strength. Across the whole market since 2016, both were followed by weaker-than-average months.
  • Volume or the jump? These are big up days (a typical 5–6% move). A follow-up check found that 5%+ jumps on ordinary volume lagged about as much in 2016–22, so the price jump may matter more than the volume. A separate study, with rules fixed first, would settle it.
  • "Maybe" means "pointed the same way at most time spans and wasn't ruled out", not "promising". Quiet buying and huge volume on a falling day missed the 2016–22 bar but leaned negative in 2023–26.
  • Same days, other stocks. Every signal is compared with random other stocks on the same dates, so a rising or falling market can't make a signal look good or bad.
  • Six tests at once. One could have passed the 2016–22 rules by luck. The 2023–26 check is the guard, and both winners passed it.
