Volume or the jump?

Study 0004 found that stocks lagged after big up days on heavy volume. Big one-day jumps often give back ground anyway, so here every heavy-volume day is compared only with days that jumped by the same amount, in the same month, on a stock of similar size, but on ordinary volume.

The answer

For huge-volume up days, the volume is not what goes with the lag: same-size jumps on ordinary volume lagged even more. Whether huge volume actually helps is not settled, because the two methods disagree. For breakouts the answer is borderline.

Does the size of the jump matter?

The one-month gap for each jump size. The huge-volume edge sits mostly in +8 to +12% jumps; breakouts swing from one period to the next.

How to read this

  • "pts" means percentage points: the gap between two returns. If heavy-volume jumps returned −0.8% in a month (against the typical stock that day) and matched ordinary-volume jumps −1.9%, the gap is +1.1 pts.
  • Like with like. Each heavy-volume day is compared only with ordinary-volume days in the same calendar month, with a jump of the same size band and a company of similar size (trading turnover, ranked that day). Days with no such match are left out and counted.
  • Two methods. The matched comparison looks at the extremes (5× volume against under 1.5×, on +3% jumps). The regression uses every up day and treats volume as a sliding scale. Q1 needed both to agree; they didn't, so it is "Not settled".
  • "The jump explains it" means the gap was under 0.3 pts in both periods. For breakouts it was −0.29 and +0.07: only just under the line. Leaving out a stock's own repeat days from its comparison moves it to −0.30, over the line. Read it as "no clear difference across the whole market", not "volume plays no part".
  • Bigger stocks may behave differently. Among today's ~750 bigger stocks, heavy-volume breakouts lagged light ones in both periods. That needs its own study, with size measured on each day, before anyone acts on it.
  • Facts, not tips. These are typical results over thousands of cases; plenty of single stocks did the opposite.
